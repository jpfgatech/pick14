## Understanding

Instruction 01 studies the *public pool* as a shared resource.
Three complementary statistics are requested:

### 1. Lifecycle distribution
How long (in rounds) does a card stay in the public pool before being matched?

- **Unit:** 1 round = N players each taking one MATCH turn.
- **Minimum lifecycle:** 1/N — card played by player k, matched immediately by player k+1.
- **Lifecycle = 1:** card played by player k and matched by player k on their NEXT turn.
- Bins are in [n, n+1) rounds to allow comparison across player counts.

**Tracking:** match_tick increments at the START of every MATCH phase.
When a card enters the pool (via PLAY, which follows a MATCH at tick T), its
`tick_in = T`.  When matched at tick T', `lifecycle_ticks = T' − T`,
`lifecycle_rounds = lifecycle_ticks / n_players`.
Initial public card dealt at game start gets `tick_in = 0`.

### 2. Digit availability probability
At each player's MATCH turn, which digits are present in the pool?
`P_avail[digit] = count(MATCH turns where digit was in pool) / count(total MATCH turns)`

### 3. Probability of loss (Ploss)
Given a digit is in the pool at player k's MATCH turn AND player k does not match it
(either by choice — matched something else — or forced pass):
`Ploss[digit] = P(digit gone at player k's NEXT MATCH turn | available now, not matched now)`

**Tracking:** after each MATCH phase, all remaining pool digits become pending observations
for the acting player. They are resolved when that player's next MATCH turn begins.

---

## Design choices

- **Strategy:** GFP match (`greedy_for_public_match`) + caution play (`caution_play`,
  plays highest game-value card first). This is the rl.md §1.5 baseline and avoids
  random-play noise while still being deterministic and reproducible.
- **Player counts:** 2p, 3p, 4p simulated separately; results overlaid for comparison.
- **Games:** 10 000 per player count (configurable via `--games`).
- Cards unmatched at game end are recorded separately (censored; excluded from matched-only
  lifecycle distribution but counted in a separate "never matched" figure).

## Expected outcomes (from 01.md)

| Expectation | Why |
|---|---|
| High-value cards rarely enter pool | caution_play keeps low-digit cards; GFP matches high-point public cards fast |
| Kings/Queens linger longest in pool | caution_play plays them first; hard to match (need 5+joker or two small cards) |
| Lifecycle is NOT exponential | Match probability increases over time (complementary cards get drawn / pool thins) |
| Availability varies a lot by digit | 9s/5s abundant; Aces scarce |
| Ploss more consistent across digits | Once in pool, loss rate depends more on opponent hand composition than card identity |

## Outputs

| File | Content |
|---|---|
| `lifecycle_dist.png` | Histogram + median-per-digit line chart (2p/3p/4p) |
| `availability_ploss.png` | Availability and Ploss bar charts by digit (2p/3p/4p) |
| `lifecycle_by_card_{N}p.png` | **13-row** per-card lifecycle distributions, x-axis aligned, one file per player count |
| `log.txt` | Numerical table + expectations check |

The 13-row figure (`lifecycle_by_card`) shows all 54 cards individually.
Each row = one digit group. Within a row, each of the 4 (or 6 for digit 5/joker)
cards is plotted as a step histogram with suit-based colour. The x-axis is shared
across all rows (`sharex=True`), so lifecycle scales are directly comparable.

## Strategy implication (noted for future instructions)

The instruction outlines a priority formula using Ploss to decide which of several
match options to take — comparing risk of losing the option vs point gain.
This is not implemented here (01 is data-only), but the Ploss tables produced are
directly reusable for any future priority-match strategy.
