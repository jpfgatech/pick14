# Decompose the value of a match, continued (fake miss-draw)

## Background

In 02-2 we injected *extra* draw events on pass turns (fake-draw) to test
whether the draw benefit is real.  Here we do the opposite: we *remove* the
extra-draw selection benefit from some match turns (fake miss-draw).

## Fake miss-draw design

After a match, the normal flow is:
  draw to n_hand+1 → play 1 (choose best from n_hand+1) → end with n_hand

Fake miss-draw replaces this with:
  draw to n_hand → play 1 (choose from n_hand, not n_hand+1) → draw 1 random → end with n_hand

The player still ends with n_hand cards and their match score is unaffected.
The only difference: they chose which card to play from n_hand options (not n_hand+1),
and the final card is a random draw rather than a selected keep.

This is applied randomly (p=0.5 per match, p=0.5 candidate per player).

In the time-series:
  Match[n] = 1  (match happened, score unchanged)
  Draw[n]  = 0  (extra selection benefit was removed)

## Comparison

Run all three variants side by side:
  original    — Draw[n] = Match[n]
  fake-draw   — Draw[n] = Match[n] ∨ pseudo-match[n]  (from 02-2)
  fake-miss-draw — Draw[n] = Match[n] with some 1s set to 0

## Expected outcome

Fake miss-draw should give a LOWER β1 estimate than both original and fake-draw.

Reason: the counterfactual in fake miss-draw (Draw[n-1]=0 after a match) has the
player still refilling to n_hand cards immediately.  In contrast, in original or
fake-draw, Draw[n-1]=0 corresponds to a true pass where the player kept a lower-quality
hand for longer before refilling.  The cost of "no extra draw" is smaller in the
fake miss-draw world, so β1 appears smaller.

## Findings

Simulated 10 000 games × {2, 3, 4} players, three variants side by side (3-lag model).

**β1 estimates**

| Variant | 2p | 3p | 4p |
|---|---|---|---|
| original | 0.206 | 0.216 | 0.212 |
| fake-draw | 0.199 | 0.207 | 0.212 |
| **fake-miss-draw** | **0.130** | **0.128** | **0.129** |

All three expectation checks PASS across all player counts.

Key results:
- **fake-miss-draw β1 ≈ 0.13**, roughly 38 % lower than original/fake-draw (~0.21), exactly as predicted.
- **original ≈ fake-draw** confirmed again (diff < 0.01).
- The three variants bracket the true causal effect of an extra-draw selection benefit:
  - **lower bound**: fake-miss-draw (~0.13) — mild counterfactual (still refills to n_hand)
  - **point estimate**: original / fake-draw (~0.21) — true pass as counterfactual
- **Base is higher** in fake-miss-draw (~0.58 vs ~0.50–0.53): removing the selection benefit from some match turns introduces Draw=0 events that are not as bad as a true pass, so the model absorbs this into a higher unconditional match rate.
- The sanity-check approximation `Base + Base·β1 + Base·β1·β2 ≈ ratio` is *more accurate* for fake-miss-draw (diff < 0.01) because the smaller β1 makes the geometric series converge faster.
