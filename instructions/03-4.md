## Math Model

In 03-4, we focus on two items

- There could be 13 useful unique sumes, 1 to 14
- We assume that the first occurance of a sum is important
- Where as more occurances contributes less
- Therefore this is a linear equation set with 13 * 2 + 1 unknown
- For each snapshot, we compute the coefficient of:
- Whether a first case of 1-13 sum set appears in handcard
- How many occurances of each sum appears

E.g. for a hand of A/4/5
- We cound 1 for the value of unique sum of A/4/5/6/9/10
- coefficients on the occurance would be 1/1/2/1/1/1 because A+4 = 5
- We keep a bias term, although it should converge to zero

With the linear model, explain the point gap in the next 1 and 2 rounds respectively

Show R^2 of prediction and value of each variable

For another regression analysis, expand the unknowns to 13 * 4 + 1
For each unique sum, the coefficients to the third unknown keeps track of whether a target appears in the public card, and its point
The forth unique sume denotes the second highest point of the available target cards
i.e. the known catagories

E.g. with a hand of A/4/5, a public pool of 9H/9C/10C would be documented as
- x3[4/5] = 1/4 (1 point on 10C which can be captured by a 4, and 4 points on 9H which can be captured by a sum of 5)
- x4[4/5] = 0/1 (No second target of 10 for 4, and 1 points on 9C which can be captured by a sum of 5 as a backup)

Show R^2 of prediction and value of each variable

## Findings

Setup: 20k games, 4p, n_hand=3, seed=0. Pre-draw snapshots (same simulation as
03-3). OLS via `numpy.linalg.lstsq`. Details: `parsing/03-4.md`.
Implementation: `scripts/03-4_linear_regression.py`.

### R² summary

| Model | Horizon | n | R² | RMSE |
|-------|---------|---|-----|------|
| A (27 feat) | N+1 | 1,035,869 | **0.1123** | 2.457 |
| A | N+2 | 926,277 | 0.0423 | 2.491 |
| B (53 feat) | N+1 | 1,035,869 | **0.1168** | 2.450 |
| B | N+2 | 926,277 | 0.0433 | 2.490 |

### Key observations

- **Adding x3/x4 (actual pub scores for known categories) barely helps**: Model B
  gains only ~0.005 R² over A.  Hand combinatorics (`x1`, `x2`) carry most
  signal.

- **Dominant positive terms: `x1[s=1..3]`** — having a hand that can make low
  sums (+1.27, +1.02, +0.80) strongly predicts good N+1 gaps.  Low sums are rare
  so their binary indicator is highly informative.

- **`x2[s=10..12]` are negative** (~−0.25 to −0.37): more high-s subsets is
  slightly *bad* on average — high-s snapshots come from large hands (B2
  post-match) where the next-turn gap is smaller.

- **N+2 R² ≈ 0.04**: the point-gap signal decays quickly; most predictability is
  in the immediate next round.
