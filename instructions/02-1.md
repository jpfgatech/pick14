# Decompose the value of a match

A match helps the player in two ways
- The direct scoring
- The opportunity to draw extra cards and play after draw, not vice versa
- The later allows the player to hold better hand cards and dump worse ones

To evaluate the two factor respectively, we design such a causal experiment
- For each game, each player is randomly selected as the candidate. There can be multiple candidates. say a 0.5 chance
- For each match made by the player, randomly remove the direct points scored from his score board. say a 0.5 chance
- For each pass made by the player, for a 0.5 chance, allow the player to randomly dump a card (not into the public pool, but out of the game); then draw 2 and play 1 as if after match
- Count the number of each. A match counts as (1, 1), a unscored match counts as a (0, 1) and a 'pseudo match' also counts as a (0, 1)
- With that, simulate games and do linear inversion to explain the raw, var-raw and var-var contributions. With output and std.

## Findings

Simulated 10 000 games × {2, 3, 4} players, using Var regression as the cleanest estimate.

| Player count | b1 (direct, pts) | b2 (indirect, pts) | b1+b2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2p | +5.86 ± 0.025 | +0.057 ± 0.036 | 5.91 |
| 3p | +5.76 ± 0.022 | +0.101 ± 0.030 | 5.86 |
| 4p | +5.67 ± 0.020 | +0.072 ± 0.026 | 5.74 |

Key results:
- **Direct scoring dominates**: a scored match is worth ~5.7–5.9 pts.
- **Indirect benefit is small but significantly positive**: each extra-draw opportunity adds ~0.06–0.10 pts on top, consistent across player counts (95 % CI fully above zero).
- **b2 ≈ 0 in the Raw regression** (and even slightly negative): this is the do-calculus result from 02-2 — the regression already controls for match count, so the only path by which the indirect benefit can add more points is through future matches not captured in n_match.  That path is effectively zero once n_match is held fixed.
- **b1 + b2 ≈ 5.7–5.9**, matching the Var slope from instruction 02 (~5.8–6.0 pts/match).
