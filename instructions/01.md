# Analysis of Public Pool



## Background
Consider the public pool of a game
From one player's perspective, the state of public pool transfers from one turn to another
In turn N+1, one new card will add to the public pool, due to play action by opponent, unless deck exhausted
In turn N+1, one old card may be removed from the public pool, due to match action by opponent
Specifically, when multiple cards of the same digit exists in the public pool, the card with the higher point has the priority to be matched away

## Math Model

We are interested in the lifecycle of a card in the public pool
As well as the availability of a digit in the public pool
Given simulated game records
- Count the distribution of lifecycle of a card in public pool:
- The minimum lifecycle would be 1/N, N being number of player
- i.e. player by player k and immediately matched by player k+1
- a lifecycle of 1 means that the card is played by player k and matched by himself after one full round
- For different number of players, accumulate the lifecycle in [n, n+1) to compare against each other

Given simulated game records
- Count the probability of availability of each digit
- Count the probability of loss
- Ploss means the probability, if a card is not matched by player k in his match turn, either spontaneously or forced to pass
- being matched away by other players before his next round

## Conditions

The player's strategy deeply affects the data sampling
Use baseline play and match strategy for data collection

## Expectations

### Data sparsity
Cards of highest value seldom enters the public pool. data may be too sparse to give stable lifecycle/probability

### Life cycle plot

- Cards of lowest value, e.g. King and Queen of club, are likely to stay in public pool for long time
- The life cycle distribution of a card is not exponential. In other words, probability of loss is not constant
- If a card is not matched for some time, it is probably because its match card, e.g. 5s for a 9, are still in deck, thus its probability of loss increases over time

### Probabilities

- Probability of availability between digits differs a lot
- Probability of loss is probably more consistent

### Strategy implication

These data may enable more advanced match strategy
Current strategy would greedily maximize the point/public point scored for current match action
However, a match option left untouched might still be available in the next turn

E.g. a public pool, with 9 of spade, 10 of Heart, and 10 of spade
Player has 5 of heart and 4 of heart in hand
Baseline strategy would go for 10 of heart, using 4 of heart to maximize points/public points
However, skipping the 10 of Heart loses 1 points at most, since 10 of spade will be available the next turn
skipping 9 of spade may cost 7 points: 9 of spade being matched away, and 5 of heart stuck in hand
counting public points only, skipping 9 of spade is still risking 3 points

Therefore, with multiple match options available, the priority should be computed by:
(in a 2 player game)
- For the target public card, check its digit.
- The target public card is always the one with the higheset point among the same digits
- Check the second highest point card of the same digit (if none, fill with 0)
- lower Risk is the point gap between 1st and 2nd option, multiplied by probability of loss
- higher Risk is the point gap, plus the point of the corresponding hand card set if there's no second option, multiplied by the probabiltiy of loss
- Ture Risk is somewhere in between
- Options with low risk should not be considered.
